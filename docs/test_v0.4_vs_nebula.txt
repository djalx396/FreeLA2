FreeLA2 against the Nebula OPTO-2A, measured on Chris's REAPER captures (2026-11-24)
====================================================================================

1) The Mac build is the build that was tested
   Chris rendered FreeLA2 v0.3.2 (Apple Silicon, REAPER 7.61) with the FreeLA2 capture kit: 212 null-test renders
   + 14 with FILTER CLEAN 2x (ANALOG and LINEAR). Every render was replayed through the reference build here with the
   exact parameter values REAPER read back. Mac vs reference: median -116 dB, worst -81 dB (24-bit render floor;
   the worst cases are the Preamp at full DRIVE, where the two processors round slightly differently).
   REAPER restarts the plug-in at the start of every render (prepareToPlay), so each render starts from rest.
   The Nebula renders kept part of the previous render's gain reduction (about 0.2-0.35 dB at the start).

3) What changed in v0.4
   The Leveler's attack wobbled: the gain-cell ripple model (low-note distortion while compressing) also reacted to
   every onset, so the gain reduction overshot and dipped by 2-4 dB in the first 50 ms. Example, 1 kHz burst
   -30 -> 0 dBFS at 8 dB setting, gain reduction (dB) after the onset:
      ms          2      5      7     10     15     20     25     30     40     50    100
      Nebula   3.59   6.89   8.35   9.73  11.03  11.75  12.17  12.40  12.70  12.91  13.66
      v0.3.2   4.65   9.95  10.28   8.92   8.08  11.19  14.98  16.79  14.78  12.77  15.14
      v0.4     4.22   8.51   9.99  11.29  12.36  12.91  13.25  13.48  13.84  14.15  15.20
   Now the ripple only works while the input level is steady; steady tones are unchanged. A refit of the timing and
   a steeper bass split in the detector were tried as well and not kept (better on some passes, worse on others).

4) What is left (see README, Known limits): Pre 2nd-harmonic phase at high DRIVE; the Leveler's grab out of very
   quiet passages and on bass from silence (the Nebula is slower); drum hits (FreeLA2 grabs the start harder, lets go
   sooner); the Clipper at 20 dB of DRIVE and more (the Nebula's clipper is frequency dependent and shifts phase there).
